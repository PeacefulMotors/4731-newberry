The building

This is a two-story brick four-family in Lewis Place, City Block 4552. Four stacked flats, kitchens on the alley wall, a decorative parapet hiding a flat roof. Lewis Place itself was laid out in 1890 and listed on the National Register in 1980.

It housed families for most of a century. Later it sat empty. The City’s land bank held it, then Prop NS rebuilt the roof, rafters, some floor structure, and tuckpointing in May 2021 — about $41,825 of work that is already in the brick. That is why this one is still a building and not a lot.

Now, and after

The houses next door

Stand on the sidewalk and you see empty grass on both sides of this building. Those lots used to hold neighbors. Across the street, another four-family of the same type is already gone.

4734–4736 Newberry Terrace

Same block, same kind of building: a two-story brick four-family. City demolition permit dated September 26, 2007 — “HANDWRECK 2 STORY 4 FAMILY BRICK HOUSE.” Work finished that November. The assessor now lists the parcel as a vacant lot, land only, appraised about $4,500.

No clean historic photograph of that house turned up in the City file online. From the permit it was the twin type to this one: brick, two stories, four flats. This building was not given that wrecking order. The City held the shell and then paid for a roof instead.

How it works when it is finished

Four legal apartments — two on the first floor, two on the second — and a basement office that is not a fifth dwelling. Each kitchen opens onto the stacked rear stairs, so every unit keeps a back way out. The office gets a street door, the side foundation door, and an alley door.

What it is worth
