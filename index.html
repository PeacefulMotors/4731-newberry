What this used to be

4731–4733 Newberry Terrace was built in 1906 as a brick four-family in the Lewis & Marshall Addition, City Block 4552. Two stories over a full basement. Four stacked flats, a center porch, and a decorative parapet hiding a flat roof — the ordinary St. Louis four-family type on this side of town.

It sits in Lewis Place, named for the private street laid out in 1890 by the family of William J. Lewis. That street went onto the National Register of Historic Places on September 15, 1980. The neighborhood runs from Martin Luther King Drive to Delmar, Newstead to Walton. ZIP 63113. Ward 10.

This was working housing next to the oldest African American private street in St. Louis. Racial covenants tried to lock who could buy here. Neighbors and lawyers — including work associated with Robert Witherspoon and Dr. Fredda Witherspoon — fought those gates. That fight sits in the same current as Shelley v. Kraemer (1948), the St. Louis case that reached the U.S. Supreme Court and ended court enforcement of racial covenants nationwide.

The building later went vacant. In 2012 the Land Reutilization Authority took title. It sat boarded until Prop NS put a new roof on it.

What it is now — and what it can be

Today it is a stabilized vacant four-family. Boards on the windows. Vacant grass lots immediately left and right. Alley brush and a dump pile. Behind that growth: the original stacked rear stairs and the tall kitchen back doors.

In May 2021 Prop NS (NELUX Development) installed a new roof — rafters, joists, sheathing, covering — plus gutters, floor-structure repairs, tuckpointing, clean-out, and board-up. City investment about $41,825. Later sold from LRA for $5,250. Promise Zone, Opportunity Zone, and the North Central NRSA.

How this building is set up

Not a single-family house and not five apartments. It is a legal four-family plus a basement office.

Four flats

Two units on the first floor, two on the second. Street doors under the porch: 4733, 4733A, 4731, 4731. Each kitchen sits on the rear / north wall and opens onto the stacked alley stairs. Those tall rear openings are kitchen back doors. They stay doors so each unit has a second way out.

Basement office — not a fifth unit

Leasing and building office, not a dwelling. Three ways in: marked leasing door on the street, existing side foundation door, and an alley door. File it that way on the permit.

Stairs and egress

Two stair runs already exist at the rear. First-floor rear doors on the lower run. Second-floor kitchens on the upper run and small landings. Basement door under the left stair. Rebuild those runs. Do not invent a new circulation diagram.

Roof and lot

Flat deck behind the front parapet — not a pitched house roof. Solar mounts on that deck, out of the street view. Parcel about 50 by 145 feet. Street parking in front. Alley in back. Side lots only if title confirms them.

No original 1906 blueprint is online. Working reconstructed plans are in the project pack. Paper originals, if they exist, would be on City Block 4552 microfilm at City Hall Room 1.

How the work actually gets done

The City already spent the money that keeps rain out. What is left is a guts-out four-family: new MEP, rebuilt rear stairs, finished apartments, an office, site cleanup, then solar. Sweat equity covers finish work. Licensed trades go on the City permit.

1Title and tape

Confirm the two side-lot deeds before fencing them. Walk every room with a tape against the working floor plans and the sweat-equity takeoff. Do not order kitchens or stairs off photographs.

2Site and rear stairs

Dumpster in the alley. Cut the lots. Pull the dump pile. Rebuild the two rear stair runs and two small second-floor landings so every kitchen door has a legal landing. Keep the basement door under the left stair.

3Permit and licensed trades

File as a four-family rehab plus basement office. Name a licensed plumber and electrician. Building Division 314-622-3313. Electrical 314-622-4808. Gynysis Construction: stairs, landings, drywall, paint, floors, cabinets, fence, carport.

4Shell to apartments

New electrical service and unit panels. New plumbing to four kitchens, four baths, and the office. Heat and air per unit. Insulation, drywall, LVP, RTA cabinets, steel doors, paint. Number the street doors. Label the leasing door.

5Office and site

Secure all three office doors. Fence only after title. Carport off the alley if the rear yard is clear. Keep egress from kitchen doors to the alley open.

6Solar last on the new roof

Lock a one-line drawing with the licensed electrician before ordering panels. Mount on the flat deck behind the parapet. Batteries and the hybrid inverter live inside.

How the power plant is set up

Goal: keep lights, refrigeration, and the office alive through a two-week grid outage. Not generator-only. Not grid-tie-only. A hybrid.

Roof — about 15 kW

Qcells on the flat roof deck, behind the parapet. The 2021 roof is why this does not need a reroof first.

Inverter + batteries

Sol-Ark 15K hybrid inverter. 30–40 kWh of LFP batteries for nights and the first days of an outage. The inverter decides when to sell, store, or island.

Generator for the long outage

10–14 kW Generac for multi-day or two-week events after the batteries are down.

Planning, not an engineered one-line. Ameren interconnection and a City electrical permit come before hardware is ordered.

Lewis Place — what this block stands on

North-central St. Louis: MLK to Delmar, Newstead to Walton. Across Delmar is the Central West End. To the northeast is The Ville and Sumner High School (1875), first public high school for African American students west of the Mississippi. Alumni include Chuck Berry, Tina Turner, and Arthur Ashe.

Neighbors still organize: Lewis Place Historical Preservation, Fountain Park / Lewis Place meetings, National Night Out, cleanups, and a garden on North Taylor. Several other buildings on this block were also Prop NS–stabilized. This one already has the new roof.

Community
