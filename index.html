What it is now — and what it can be

The building sits boarded on Newberry Terrace with vacant grass lots immediately left and right. The City’s Prop NS program already stabilized it: new roof and rafters in May 2021, repaired floor structure, tuckpointing, gutters, and a secured shell. The alley still holds a dump pile and brush. Behind that growth are the original stacked rear stairs and the tall kitchen back doors — not windows.

What this building is

The facts

Brick four-family in the Lewis & Marshall Addition, City Block 4552. Two stories over a full basement. About 4,912 square feet on a parcel roughly 50 by 145 feet (about 7,255 square feet). Zoning is multi-family residential. Street parking plus an alley in the rear.

The roof is a St. Louis four-family flat roof behind a decorative front parapet — not a pitched house roof. That is why solar mounts on the roof deck, not on sloped shingles.

The program

Four legal apartments plus a basement leasing office. The office is not a fifth dwelling. It gets a front door, a side foundation door, and an alley door.

Each apartment kitchen sits on the rear (north) wall and opens onto the stacked alley stairs. Those tall rear openings are kitchen back doors. First-floor rear doors and the basement door under the left stair stay as egress.

What this can be

Four homes

Four legal flats in a brick building that already has a new roof. Kitchens on the alley wall, each with a real back door onto rebuilt stairs. Numbered street doors. Quiet side lots turned into one fenced yard if title allows.

A working office

Basement leasing and building office with three ways in: street, side foundation door, and alley. Not a fifth apartment. Ready for access control and outage alerts — not a fifth dwelling unit.

Power that stays on

Grid-tied hybrid solar on the flat roof: about 15 kW of Qcells, a Sol-Ark 15K hybrid inverter, 30–40 kWh of LFP batteries, and a 10–14 kW Generac for long outages. Goal: ride two weeks without the grid.

The roof work is already the expensive part of a solar-ready shell. Panels sit behind the parapet, out of the street view. Licensed electrician on the City permit. This is planning, not an engineered one-line.

Lewis Place — what this block stands on

4731 Newberry Terrace is in the Lewis Place neighborhood of north-central St. Louis: Martin Luther King Drive on the north, Delmar Boulevard on the south, Newstead on the east, Walton on the west. ZIP 63113. Ward 10.

The neighborhood takes its name from Lewis Place itself — a short private street with a center parkway, laid out in 1890 by the family of William J. Lewis. Homes along that parkway were built between 1890 and 1928. The street was placed on the National Register of Historic Places on September 15, 1980 and is also a City historic district.

Lewis Place is remembered as the oldest African American private street in St. Louis, and for the fight over who was allowed to own a home here. In the 1940s, Black St. Louisans — including work associated with attorney Robert Witherspoon and Dr. Fredda Witherspoon — challenged the racial covenants that locked the gates. That local fight sits in the same legal current as Shelley v. Kraemer (1948), the St. Louis case that went to the U.S. Supreme Court and struck down court enforcement of racial covenants nationwide.

The neighborhood lost people and buildings for decades. What remains is a mix of original brick four-families, vacant lots, and a community that is still organizing: Lewis Place Historical Preservation, Fountain Park / Lewis Place community meetings, National Night Out, cleanups, and a neighborhood garden on North Taylor that has put hundreds of pounds of produce into neighbors’ hands.

Across Delmar is the Central West End. To the northeast is The Ville, home of Sumner High School — opened in 1875 as the first public high school for African American students west of the Mississippi. Alumni include Chuck Berry, Tina Turner, and Arthur Ashe. Ranken Technical College sits just south of the neighborhood. Saint Louis University and Washington University are within a few miles.

Sources: City of St. Louis Lewis Place overview; Lewis Place Historical Preservation; National Register listing, Sept. 15, 1980.

What is around the building

Distances below are neighborhood-scale, not a surveyed map. The point is the location: north of Delmar, inside a historic district, with buses on the main streets and the Central West End a short ride south.

Transit

Metro buses run on Delmar, Taylor, and Page. Delmar is a few blocks south. Central West End MetroLink (Red and Blue lines) is the nearest rail — a short drive or a bus hop — then downtown in about ten minutes on the train. I-64 is the fast car route downtown.

Parks and green

Beckett Park (Taylor and Cook) is the neighborhood play space: playground, basketball, field, gazebo. The Lewis Place garden on North Taylor grows food for the block. Fountain Park sits next door, with the Dr. King statue. Fairground Park — about 131 acres at Grand and Natural Bridge — has a lake, pool, fields, and a walking trail. Forest Park is a short drive west.

Food and daily needs

Aldi just north of Delmar. Soll’s Gateway Market, a long-running butcher and market, in Fountain Park. Family Dollar and corner stores on the main streets. Whole Foods, Straub’s, and the Euclid restaurant strip sit in the Central West End on the other side of Delmar.

Schools and trades

Nearby SLPS options include Hickey and Cole / Pamoja, then Yeatman-Liddell and Sumner High. Ranken Technical College is the local trades campus — electrical, construction, automotive, IT.

Care and campus

BJC / Washington University Medical Center and the Central West End hospitals are the regional medical cluster, south of Delmar. Cabanne Branch Library (Carnegie, 1906) is a short trip west in the Academy neighborhood.

Community

Fountain Park–Lewis Place meetings: 10 a.m.–noon on the 4th Saturday at Westside M.B. Church, 4675 Page. Lewis Place Historical Preservation runs cleanups, youth days, and a renewable-energy conference. This building’s solar plan is meant to sit in that same story.
